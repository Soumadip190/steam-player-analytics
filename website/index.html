Chapter 1 · Where the hours go

Playtime is concentrated in a few titles.

Dota 2 leads with close to a million recorded hours, followed by Counter-Strike Global Offensive and Team Fortress 2. Engagement is dominated by a handful of long-running multiplayer games, while most of the catalogue sees far less play.

Observed in the historical dataset.
Chapter 2 · Most players barely play

Raw playtime is heavily skewed, so we transform it.

A large spike of players sits near zero hours, with a long tail of heavy players. Applying log(1 + hours) makes the distribution workable for clustering, which is why the pipeline uses a log transformation before scaling.

Motivates the log transform used in segmentation.
Chapter 3 · Buying and playing are related, loosely

More games owned tends to mean more hours, but not reliably.

The notebook measures a Pearson correlation of 0.47 and a Spearman correlation of 0.55: a moderate positive relationship. Plenty of players own few games and still log thousands of hours, so purchases alone cannot describe engagement.

Correlation only, not a causal claim.
Chapter 4 · Three behavioural segments emerge

K-Means separates players by playtime and library size.

Cluster 0 holds low-playtime players, Cluster 1 regular players, and Cluster 2 the dedicated players with the largest libraries and longest playtime. These are behavioural segments derived from the available features, not verified player types.

Test recommendation strategies across player segments with sufficient sample sizes.

Continuous Measurement

Monitor click-through rates, conversions, retention, and engagement in a real production experiment.

Business impact (illustrative)

If a properly instrumented production experiment found a conversion improvement, the value would depend on traffic, margins, and retention effects. Any forecast built from this project’s simulated lift is an illustrative assumption, not a measured business outcome.

Limitations & research integrity

  • The dataset contains historical observational behaviour.
  • It has no actual recommendation exposure or randomized assignments.
  • A/B testing outcomes were synthetically generated.
  • Clicks and conversions were generated independently, so this is not a true click-to-purchase funnel.
  • The project does not establish real causal effects on Steam users.
  • Real-world deployment would require a properly instrumented randomized experiment.
